The game

What You Are Getting Into

Every run starts simple: build towers, hold the core. Then the systems stack up. Towers, talents, Fusions, strains and bosses all feed into each other, and that is where the skill cap lives.

  • Your Base Can Fly

    Your core is not bolted down. Tap Lift Off, pick a landing spot, and the swarm reroutes to follow you. Land on a tower and the core takes its gun: ×2 damage and +1 range, while its own gun keeps firing.

  • Builds That Play Differently

    19 towers with upgrades, affixes and veteran ranks. 7 Talent trees, each with a Capstone that changes how you play. Put 8 points into two trees to open their Fusion. There are 21, like Hot Rounds: Kinetic hits set enemies on fire.

  • A Swarm That Adapts

    Every few waves the swarm reads your defense and sends a strain built to beat it. You get one wave of warning, and selling is free until it lands. Whatever ends your run comes back next time as your Nemesis.

  • Two Enemy Families

    The hive wants the core's energy to feed on: Gnawers, Clutches that burst into Swarmers, Nurses that heal the pack, Matrons that drop broods. From Wave 8 the Remnant join them, the war machines of every pilot before you, still running. Elites come Overgrown, covered in glowing spines.

  • Thirteen Bosses

    A boss arrives every 10 waves, with phases, powers and its own theme. Five are hive monsters, like the Titan, whose shell breaks off as you hit it. The other eight are stranger, like Quackzilla, the Immortal Snail, The Auditor and Leia, my cat.

  • Endless Waves

    A run lasts until your core falls. Wormholes open new roads, and the wave before one lands is a checkpoint: 90 seconds to move towers, rebuild and respec for free. 21 Anomalies trade power for risk, like Glass Sky: +35% damage, but no core shield.

  • Send the Probe

    Your probe is a small ship of your own. Send it to wreckage, gas clouds and asteroids, and it brings the salvage home by itself. Fly it into a storm to harvest an ability charge: an Ion Storm gives you a Strike, a Cryo Front a Stasis.

  • The Map Fights Back

    Storms drift across the map. Each one boosts an element and hits the enemies under it: Ion Storms throw lightning, Solar Flares set them burning. A Black Hole eats your towers. Feed it the swarm and it bursts into a White Hole that leaves a Newborn Star.

  • Daily Hunt

    One map a day. Every pilot gets the same talent build and the same big dice rolls, so the best play wins. Your first run counts. Every bounty you claim is a choice: Extract and end the run as a win, or push on for ×1.25 score. It opens at Level 20.

  • Ranks That Take Time

    Every run feeds your career rank, from Recruit to Fleet Admiral and then up through the star classes to Keeper of the Gradient. The high ranks also need days served, so nobody rushes to the top. Supernova resets your talents for a permanent bonus, a new emblem and a wider core light.

  • Music That Writes Itself

    The soundtrack is composed live while you play, so it is never the same twice. Each of the 13 bosses has its own theme. Open the Music Studio to change the genre, mix the parts or write your own tracks. See the Studio.

  • Training Shows You How

    Basic Training is one guided run. It teaches the core, the probe, Lift Off and your first boss as each one comes up. Advanced Training covers the deeper systems, and the Field Manual explains everything else when you want the detail.

The music

The Music Studio

There is no playlist. The game composes its music live from 10 genres and 40 styles, and every song gets its own name. It follows the fight: when the swarm gets heavy, the music builds with it. Keep the songs you like, shape the sound, or write your own.

From the developer

Why I Made It

I'm not a coder, and I'm not an artistic person. I'm a gamer with a really good understanding of game mechanics, and I had an idea: a game made for the players.

So I built Last Gradient together with AI. We worked on the code, the art and the music side by side. The decisions were mine, and I closely criticized every output until it felt right. No studio and no team, just me chasing what makes a game fun.

I'm not here to get rich. I have a day job. I'm tired of the predatory games on the market today. You know the microtransaction-riddled crap I'm talking about. Last Gradient has none of it: you pay once, and the whole game is yours.

This game serves as a letter to the games of old, the ones you just played and enjoyed. The games that developers spent time on to make great. I want to bring that feeling back and leave the corporate slop behind.

I wanted a game anyone can pick up and play offline, while waiting in line or to unwind after a long day, with no ads in the way. Just you, the swarm and one more wave.

And this is only the start. I plan to take player feedback and keep making the game better. Send Feedback is built into the game, and I read the reports. Thanks for being here.

– Nic
